# Fódhla

What is known about Fódhla fits in the margins of the institution he founded.

In the rare documents that name him, he is credited as the founder of the Registry. The Registry existed as his idea before it existed as anything else. Its Ledger — the intelligence archive operatives consult to know the ground before they stand on it — came later, built by his husband [[Marcus Halberstram]]. Fódhla gave the Registry its purpose. Marcus gave it its memory.

The Registry takes contracts. It does not ask what the contract is for, or who is asking. A senator and a criminal walk in with the same offer; the Registry evaluates both on the same terms: can you pay, will you honor the agreement. That is all. Political standing, reputation, history — none of it enters the calculation.

That philosophy is Fódhla's. He built the institution on it and has never seen reason to revise it.

People who spend years in the Registry may never encounter him. He does not attend briefings. He does not run operations. When he does appear — silver-haired, unhurried, looking at something just past whoever is speaking to him — people notice. Not because of ceremony; there is none. But no one who has been there forgets it. Something in the Registry was about to change.

The contracts that bind operatives to the Registry are not a formality. They carry weight most people cannot lift. Fódhla can decide otherwise. No one else can. He does not do it often. When he does, it is not a negotiation.

His private life is not in the record. He is close to [[Marcus Halberstram]] in a way he is close to no one else. The weight those agreements carry comes from Marcus; what Fódhla can do with that weight is a separate matter. The Ledger does not go into it. What Fódhla keeps to himself, he tells Marcus.
